# Changes

## 1.01

- Bubbles are single round bubbles, and there are more of them: one shuts
  the way, others stand about, and one may cork a vent in the flask's own
  glass, holding the water in. One stroke a flask pops every bubble the
  finger passes over; the rest stay.
- The way through is played only when asked, from the Demo key in the top row or
  from the card, at any moment and at the game's own pace; a tap stops it.
  A flask is never taken out of the player's hands.
- A flask that has set and gone still ends; a stroke over its bubbles still counts as play.
- Tones are materials now: glass, brass and stone, with their rooms. Water
  is the same in every tone.
- A long result no longer runs off the edge of the card.

## 1.00

The first release.

- A round glass flask with a maze of rings inside. Catch the rain in the
  neck, turn the flask with one finger, and pour the water out of the
  bottom; what counts is the longest single pour.
- Five tiers of knottiness, dealt shuffled rather than climbing, in three
  styles of maze: scattered gaps, spirals, and chambers that hold water
  like cups.
- Troubles, two at most on a flask: a count of turns, a clock, bubbles of
  air to pop, seeds that open into a rose, clover or daisy across a
  corridor, floats that shut a gap above or below, a flask that turns by
  itself, a flask that flips like a coin, snow, lava, slime, and flasks that
  breathe and change their maze.
- A flask poured poorly is played back to show one way through.
- Music written as it plays, never the same twice, or silence.
- Three tones, sea, amber and moss, each with a day and a night face.
- English, Finnish and Russian, and any other language from a plain text
  module.
- A page about the game inside it, behind the `?` key, in every language
  the game speaks.
- No permissions, no network.
