How it plays

Water falls from above in ten volumes. Keep the neck of the flask up and catch it.

Turn the flask with one finger, the way a hand turns a dial. Inside are rings of glass with gaps in them and short walls between: water lies wherever its corridor is lowest, and slips through a gap when the gap comes underneath.

Lead it down to the bottom opening and let it go. What counts is one pour: every drop that leaves with no pause longer than a blink. Gather first, then open the way, and the number under the flask climbs.

Water that sneaks in through the bottom turns grey and never counts. The bottom is a way out, not a way in.

Never the same flask twice

Every flask is a new maze, from gentle (two rings, wide gaps, a short way down) to knotty (four rings, narrow gaps, a long way round). Five beads at the top tell how knotty the flask in hand is. The flasks do not climb a ladder: every five of them hold each tier once, shuffled, and two hard ones never stand side by side.

The mazes come in three styles: scattered gaps, a spiral of gaps that asks to be turned all the way round, and chambers walled off beside the gaps, which hold water like cups.

What can happen

Some flasks keep a trouble or two. Each is said under the flask as it is handed over, and no flask carries more than two.

A count of turns

The flask turns only so far, then sets fast. The count is the least the maze needs, and a good deal more.

A clock

The flask turns only for so long. Time enough for the rain and the way down, if the hand is sure.

Bubbles

Round bubbles of air: across a corridor, in a gap, or in the flask's own glass. One stroke a flask pops every bubble the finger passes over; the rest stay. Some shut the way; the one in the glass holds the water in.

Seeds

Pearls stuck to the glass. Water runs past them, but a pearl wetted enough opens into its flower across the corridor: a red one into a rose, a green one into clover, a golden one into a daisy.

A float

A bob on a rail between two gaps. Water lifts it to shut the gap above; when the water drains away, it sinks and shuts the gap below.

A wild flask

It turns by itself. A finger holds it like a rubber band, and it slips loose when pulled too hard.

A coin flip

Now and then the flask flips over like a coin, water and all, and left becomes right.

Snow

Snow that meets water in the glass freezes it. The ice turns with the glass until it melts.

Lava

Lava boils the water it meets into steam, and the steam rises out of the neck.

Slime

On flasks with a clock, slime makes the water thick, sticky and slow.

A breathing flask

A plain flask may breathe: once its rain is over, the glass swells a little and the maze inside becomes another.

Whenever you ask, at any moment of a flask, the game plays it for you from the start, at its own pace, along one way through; a tap stops it. A flask is never taken out of your hands. A new flask is one key away at any time, and the ? key opens a page inside the game that tells all of this, with no network needed.

Look and sound

Three tones, each a stuff for the maze and a room around it: glass, brass and stone, each with a day face and a night face that follow the phone. Water, bubbles and flowers stay the same in all of them.

glass brass stone

The music is written as it plays: slow drops of a plucked, glassy voice on a pentatonic scale, a new piece each time the last one ends, so it never comes round again. Or silence.

Languages

English, Finnish and Russian, inside the game. It starts in the phone's own language when it has it, and one key in the top row changes it at any time. Any other language can be added as a small module of plain text. The same key saves the language spoken now as a file, blank in English, filled in Finnish or Russian; fill it in with any text editor, the English above each line, and load it back with the same key. A blank template is also in the repository.

Small and quiet

No permissions, no network, no advertising, no accounts. The record stays on the phone.
